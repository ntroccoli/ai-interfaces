# ArtifactsBench has an LLM judge score generated web artifacts from screenshots and a checklist; the expert check covers a small subset

Tencent's Hunyuan team built a benchmark of 1,825 queries, each asking a model for a working
visual artifact such as a game, an SVG poster or a web app, and had over 30 models answer them
([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S2.SS1)). A browser renders every answer and takes
three screenshots; a multimodal LLM judge, mainly Gemini 2.5 Pro, then scores the code and the
screenshots against a ten-item checklist written for that query
([method](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S3)). The judge agreed with front-end experts on
90.95% of answer pairs, but that figure is the best of fifteen judge setups, all tried on the same
280 queries from 6 models; the open judge reached 71.34%
([ablation](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS3)). Its model ranking matched WebDev Arena's
human votes at 94.4% (normalized Footrule) over 12 models, though the ranks in that chart do not
follow the paper's own main table ([arena](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS4)).

## Source

A paper on arXiv by the Tencent Hunyuan team (twenty authors, all at Tencent but one each from
NJU, PKU and an independent researcher). The first version (v1) was posted on 2025-07-07; this
case was written from v2 of 2025-09-29, read in HTML form on 2026-11-14
([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04952)). Licence: CC0 (public domain dedication). arXiv gives no change note;
comparing the two versions, v2 adds the leaderboard under the stable Gemini judge (with GPT-5), a
check that the preview and stable Gemini judges order models alike, harness details and a
30%, 40% and 30% target for the difficulty tiers (v1 gave the same split with score thresholds), moves the limitations to the appendix, and turns the abstract's
"over" claim for expert agreement into an "up to" claim. **What the authors sell:** Tencent offers
the Hunyuan models; the paper ranks two of them (Hunyuan-Turbos-Preview and Hunyuan-A13B) and used
Hunyuan-Turbos to draft the checklists, so it is a lab ranking its own models. The paper shows its
tables, its prompts and its agreement metric; the project page and its linked repository release
the queries, checklists, judge outputs and scripts
([project page](https://artifactsbenchmark.github.io/)).

## What was made or studied

A benchmark for code that must look right and respond to input, not only run. Queries came from
showcases by experts, public SVG and web-snippet datasets, case studies on the web and screenshots
turned into prompts by an LLM; filters removed duplicates, near-identical renders and items with
no interaction, though purely static items such as SVG posters were kept on purpose; experts and an LLM rewrote the prompts
([pipeline](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S2.SS2)). The 1,825 queries span nine topics, led
by web apps (441 queries) and games (413 queries) ([statistics](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S1)),
and are split after the fact into easy, medium and hard tiers by how 30+ models scored on them
([tiers](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S2.SS2)). Each query carries a checklist of 10 items,
five about what shows on screen (layout, fidelity, motion, feedback, clarity) and five about the
code (logic, robustness, modularity, performance, redundancy), each worth 10 points
([checklists](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S3.SS1)). Models answer in one turn; the answer
is rendered and judged as is.

## Who did what

- **AI:** the ranked LLMs wrote every artifact (`build`). Multimodal LLMs acted as judges
  (`judge`): a preview build, `Gemini-2.5-Pro-Preview-0506`, for the main table; Gemini-2.5-Pro
  (stable) for the later table; and Qwen2.5-VL-72B, an open-weights model, as second judge
  ([referees](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S3.SS2)). GPT-o4-mini and Qwen3-32B-Instruct
  were tried only in the ablations ([ablation](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS3)). LLMs
  also helped build the data: Hunyuan-Turbos drafted the checklists, Gemini-2.5-Pro sorted queries
  into categories and interaction levels ([classification](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS7)),
  an LLM rewrote prompts ([pipeline](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S2.SS2)) and a prompt
  scored them for quality ([filtering](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS6)). The harness section credits sub-categories to a lightweight classifier; the appendix gives that job to Gemini-2.5-Pro, and v1 to Hunyuan-Turbos ([classification](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS7), [v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v1#S2.SS3)).
- **Rule-based tools:** Playwright driving headless Chromium at a fixed window size with fixed
  seeds, MinHash and similarity filters, and perceptual hashing of screenshots
  ([harness](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S2.SS2)).
- **People:** the authors; experts who rewrote prompts and refined the LLM-drafted checklists;
  annotators who hand-made a 10% seed of checklists and agreed at 0.8 Cohen's kappa or above
  ([checklists](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S2.SS2)); several engineers with front-end
  experience who scored the 280-query validation set blind, the median of their ratings being the
  reference ([experts](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS3)); and the public voters behind
  WebDev Arena.

## How it was checked

Two checks against people. First, the experts' scores on 280 randomly picked queries, each
answered by 6 models, were compared with the judge's: for every query, each pair of answers counts
as a hit when judge and experts agree on which is better, and Pair ACC is the share of hits. Four
judges were tried in fifteen setups (with or without screenshots, with text descriptions instead,
with or without the code), and the best was kept
([ablation](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS3)). The experts worked blind to the judge's
output, on samples shown in shuffled order
([experts](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS3)). The first version names the judge in
that study as Gemini-2.5-pro-0506 ([v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v1#S3)). Second, the
full-benchmark ranking was compared with WebDev Arena's vote-based ranking by normalized Footrule
distance, and the same was done for WebBench as a baseline
([arena](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS4)). The two judges' orderings and the preview
and stable Gemini versions were compared only in charts
([cross-judge](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS3)).

## What worked

- Screenshots made the judge agree with experts more often: Gemini-2.5-pro rose from 79.06%
  without an image to 87.10% with one and 90.95% with several; GPT-o4-mini and Qwen2.5-VL-72B rose
  along the same steps ([ablation](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS3)).
- The code mattered as well: with screenshots alone Gemini fell to 74.94%, and text descriptions
  of the screenshots beat no image but trailed the screenshots themselves
  ([ablation](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS3)).
- The judged ranking tracked public human votes far more closely than WebBench did (94.4% against
  69.4%) ([arena](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS4)).
- The authors report that the two judges order models in largely the same way, with differences
  among the top models, and that the stable Gemini orders models exactly as its retired preview
  did ([cross-judge](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS3)); the models found in both
  Gemini tables (9 models by exact name, 10 models if the preview and stable Gemini count as one) do keep the same order ([tables](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS5)).
- The authors' findings about the models, which their tables only partly bear out (see below):
  instruction-tuned generalists beat coder and vision variants of the same family; scores rise
  with model size and with longer reasoning, though short answers can still score well; vision
  and code scores move together ([results](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS2),
  [length](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS3)).

## What went wrong

- The headline agreement rests on a small subset: 280 queries out of 1,825 queries, answered by
  6 models out of more than 30 models, and the 90.95% is the best of fifteen setups tried on that
  same subset, with no held-out check ([experts](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS3),
  [ablation](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS3)).
- No agreement with experts is reported for the stable Gemini judge, which scores the later table with GPT-5 at the top (v1 names the preview build as the judge of the expert study); that table leans on a chart showing it orders models like the
  preview ([cross-judge](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS3)).
- The open judge agreed with experts on only 71.34% of pairs, yet it still scores a full
  leaderboard ([ablation](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS3)).
- By the authors' account, even the strongest judge parts from the experts, because it also
  weighs code traits that people looking at the page do not ([ablation](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS3)).
- A handful of frames cannot follow every interaction: the authors name long workflows, state that
  builds up over time and game physics as cases it may miss
  ([limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS1)).
- Only one-turn generation is tested: no model refines, debugs or iterates on its artifact
  ([limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS1)).
- The authors list the known faults of model judges: hallucination, sensitivity to the prompt,
  weak sense of code quality and drift between versions
  ([related work](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S5)). Drift happened here: the preview
  Gemini judge was retired and had to be replaced
  ([cross-judge](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS3)).
- On complex interactive tasks the two judges give different detailed scores, which the authors call minor; they suspect
  each model has its own bias when reading long interactions
  ([referees](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS5)).
- The scoring prompt renders only the last HTML or SVG block of an answer
  ([prompt](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS8)).
- The authors say all models do worst on the most interactive tasks and on management systems; no
  model in the difficulty chart passed 50 points on the hard tier
  ([results](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS2), [chart](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.F5)).
- Answer length is missing for several closed models, whose reasoning length cannot be read
  ([table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS1)).
- The capture is described three ways: before, during and after a scripted interaction in the
  harness section; at fixed intervals in the limitations; and as an adaptively scrolled page in
  the scoring prompt ([harness](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S2.SS2),
  [limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS1),
  [prompt](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS8)).
- Inconsistencies we found between the paper's text, charts and tables:
  - The Arena chart's benchmark ranks do not follow the main table: by table averages o3-mini,
    DeepSeek-R1 and Qwen3-253B would sit seventh, eighth and ninth, exactly as on the Arena, but
    the chart puts them eighth, ninth and seventh; the 94.4% is computed from the chart's ranks
    ([arena](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS4), [table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS1)).
  - The text says the benchmark ranks o3-mini a little above the human vote; the chart shows it
    one place below ([arena](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS4)).
  - Table 3 contradicts two model findings: Qwen-2.5-Coder32B (35.32 points) beats
    Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct (32.07 points); and management systems are not the lowest category for 17 of
    33 models, while the most interactive tier is not the lowest for one model, DeepSeek-R1-0528
    (51.18 points on static pages against 51.33 points on the most interactive ones)
    ([table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS1)).
  - The text puts DeepSeek-distill-qwen-32B 5 points short of Qwen3-32B; the table gives a gap of
    7.12 points ([results](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS2)).
  - Rankings are said to hold across difficulty tiers, but the tier chart shows swaps, for example
    DeepSeek-R1-0528 above seed-thinking-1.5 on easy tasks and below it on hard ones (values read
    from the chart image)
    ([tiers](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.F5)).
  - The tier split is said to meet the target of 30%, 40% and 30%, yet the middle tier holds about
    33% and the hard tier about 36% ([tiers](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S2.SS2)).
  - Gemini is called the top model overall, from the preview-judged table; under the stable Gemini
    judge GPT-5 leads, and under the Qwen judge Claude 4.0-Sonnet leads
    ([tables](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS5)).
  - Labelling slips: one table files two Claude models under open-source; the Qwen-judge table
    skips its eleventh row number; two checklist prompts are captioned as classification prompts
    ([tables](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS5),
    [prompts](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS8)).
  - The paper names the retired judge as the `0506` preview; the project page names the `0605`
    preview, and the repository uses both names ([cross-judge](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS3),
    [project page](https://artifactsbenchmark.github.io/)).
- The authors' own best model, Hunyuan-Turbos-Preview, appears only under the preview Gemini
  judge: it is absent from the stable-judge table, the Qwen-judge table, the tier chart and the
  Arena comparison, so it gets no cross-judge or human-vote check
  ([table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS1), [tables](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS5)).
- **Not reported:** how many experts scored, whether they were Tencent staff, how far they agreed
  with each other on the 280 queries, which 6 models were scored, and how ties counted in Pair
  ACC; whether the Gemini judge favours Gemini answers (`Gemini-2.5-Pro-Preview-0506` is second in
  the main table under a Gemini judge but fourth under the Qwen judge), or whether checklists drafted by
  Hunyuan-Turbos favour Hunyuan models; repeat runs or error bars, although score stability is a
  stated goal; what the scripted interaction does on each page; the cost and time of judging. The
  only date given for the WebDev Arena ranks is an access date in 2024, before several compared
  models existed, and the citation attached to WebDev Arena points to a different paper
  ([experts](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS3),
  [tables](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS5),
  [arena](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS4)).

## Practices to copy

- [stated] Render each generated page in a real headless browser and give the judge screenshots from before, during and after a scripted interaction. ([Harness](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S2.SS2))
- [stated] Give the judge the code along with the screenshots, so it can also score logic and robustness. ([Ablation](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS3))
- [stated] Pair each task with its own checklist, drafted by an LLM from hand-made seeds and edited by people, and score item by item. ([Checklists](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S2.SS2))
- [stated] Before trusting a model judge, measure its pairwise agreement with blind expert scores, and pick the judge and its inputs by that. ([Experts](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS3))
- [stated] Compare the judged ranking with an outside ranking built from human votes. ([Arena](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS4))
- [stated] Run a second judge from another vendor and compare orderings; re-check when a judge version is retired. ([Cross-judge](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS3))
- [ours] Measure judge agreement on a held-out set with more models, and again for each new judge version, before quoting it. ([Ablation](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS3))
- [ours] Never let one model family judge a leaderboard that holds its own models without a cross-judge check on the top ranks. ([Tables](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS5))
- [ours] Recompute headline agreement figures from the published tables; here the chart behind the Arena figure does not match them. ([Arena](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#S4.SS4))
- [stated] Proposed by the authors, not tried: for long interactions, check page state through the DOM or a video instead of a few frames. ([Limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04952v2#A1.SS1))

## Context

- Model era: models of 2024 and 2025, from GPT-4o and Qwen2.5 to Claude 4, Gemini 2.5 Pro and
  GPT-5, with Gemini 2.5 Pro as judge (preview, then stable) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B.
- The repository's notes say the judge moved from a retired preview (called `0605` there, though its older results are credited to `0506`) to
  Gemini 2.5 Pro (stable) on 2025-07-25 ([repo](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/ArtifactsBenchmark));
  later model releases by other labs quote their ArtifactsBench scores, so the benchmark is in use
  as a yardstick ([project page](https://artifactsbenchmark.github.io/)).
- Related: a study where Gemini-2.5-pro and GPT-4 walk app screens in place of people, also
  checked against people on a small sample,
  [synthetic cognitive walkthrough](2025-12-03-uw-synthetic-cognitive-walkthrough.md).
